TINYRTOS · BOOT LAB · 03

Make startup observable

Goal: connect the copy and clear loops you traced to two actual C variables. This is a small step toward proving your own kernel boots before you build tasks.

Recall before reading

Your startup has a data-copy loop and a BSS-clear loop. Which one prepares a global with a nonzero initial value? Explain where that value comes from. Send your reasoning in chat for feedback.

Your change

After the recall check, edit rtos/src/main.c: add two file-scope integer variables, one explicitly initialized to 7 and one without an explicit initializer. Choose their names yourself. Leave the main loop as it is.

Before building, predict each variable's value when execution first enters main. Show your teacher the code and predictions. We will review them, recover the build commands with debug information, and inspect the variables in QEMU/GDB together.

Checkpoint: adding declarations is the exercise. Observing startup in the debugger is the evidence; we have not completed that observation yet.

Read after predicting

The GNU linker manual's load-address example connects separate load and runtime addresses to a startup copy, followed by BSS clearing. Read the example and its two initialization loops; compare them with your startup.S. Your linker uses FLASH as a region name; that name alone does not establish the physical memory technology of the emulated board.

Ask your teacher followup questions whenever a step or term is unclear. We will work through one question at a time.
